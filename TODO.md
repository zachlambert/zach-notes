# How to better organise research papers?

Having an unorganised list of research papers isn't particularly useful.

Research papers are developed in a sequential manner, building on top of one-another and introducing new ideas.

Therefore, they should be stored in particular note sections for that specific concept / field of interest.

How should they be referenced and stored?
- Firstly, a paper may have a particular "introduction" section, where it is first introduced in the context of a larger topic. For example, introducing local mapping in visual slam, mentioning a paper that first explores this idea.
- We _could_ have a dedicated page for the paper which outlines the idea. However, I think a better approach is:
    - Never (or rarely) write out notes for all the details of a paper - this takes ages, let the paper stand by itself.
    - If there _are_ relevant details, explain these in the broader concept, and refer to where the concept comes from. This is also better if there are multiple papers that introduce a similar concept, so I can give my definition (of a particular equation/algorithm for example), but link against a few examples - which may have various minor differences.

For every reference to a paper, include the year of publication.

In the page a paper is first introduced, put a section at the bottom for the list of references - which gives the paper summary block and PDF (if available).

Can generate a list of papers, similar to the keywords.
